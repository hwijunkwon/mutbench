Plasticity of a critical antigenic determinant in the West Nile virus NY99 
envelope protein domain III.

West Nile virus (WNV) is a mosquito-borne flavivirus that causes febrile 
illness, encephalitis, and occasionally death in humans. The envelope protein is 
the main component of the WNV virion surface, and domain III of the envelope 
protein (EIII) is both a putative receptor binding domain and a target of highly 
specific, potently neutralizing antibodies. Envelope E-332 (E-332) is known to 
have naturally occurring variation and to be a key determinant of neutralization 
for anti-EIII antibodies. A panel of viruses containing all possible amino acid 
substitutions at E-332 was constructed. E-332 was found to be highly tolerant of 
mutation, and almost all of these changes had large impacts on antigenicity of 
EIII but only limited effects on growth or virulence phenotypes.
