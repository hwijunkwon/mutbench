A novel in vitro system of supported planar endosomal membranes (SPEMs) reveals 
an enhancing role for cathepsin B in the final stage of Ebola virus fusion and 
entry.

Ebola virus is the causative agent of Ebola virus disease, which is severe and 
frequently lethal. EBOV gains entry into cells via late endosomes/lysosomes. The 
events immediately preceding fusion of the viral and endosomal membranes are 
incompletely understood. In this study, we report a novel in vitro system for 
studying virus fusion with endosomal membranes. We validated the system by 
demonstrating the low pH dependencies of influenza and Lassa virus fusion. 
Moreover, we show that further cathepsin B action enhances the fusion activity 
of the primed Ebola virus glycoprotein. Finally, this model endosomal membrane 
system should be useful in studying the mechanisms of bilayer breaching by other 
enveloped viruses, by non-enveloped viruses, and by acid-activated bacterial 
toxins.
