Old World (Africa) and New World (South America) arenaviruses are associated 
with human hemorrhagic fevers. Efforts to develop small molecule therapeutics 
have yielded several chemical series including the 
4-acyl-1,6-dialkylpiperazin-2-ones. Herein, we describe an extensive exploration 
of this chemotype. In initial Phase I studies, R1 and R4 scanning libraries were 
assayed to identify potent substituents against Old World (Lassa) virus. In 
subsequent Phase II studies, R6 substituents and iterative R1, R4 and R6 
substituent combinations were evaluated to obtain compounds with improved Lassa 
and New World (Machupo, Junin, and Tacaribe) arenavirus inhibitory activity, in 
vitro human liver microsome metabolic stability and aqueous solubility.
