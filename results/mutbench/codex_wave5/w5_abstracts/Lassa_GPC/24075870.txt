Innate immune response to arenaviral infection: a focus on the highly pathogenic 
New World hemorrhagic arenaviruses.

Koma T(1), Huang C, Kolokoltsova OA, Brasier AR, Paessler S.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pathology and Institute for Human Infections and Immunity, 
University of Texas Medical Branch, Galveston, TX 77550, USA.

Arenaviruses are enveloped, negative-stranded RNA viruses that belong to the 
family Arenaviridae. This diverse family can be further classified into OW (Old 
World) and NW (New World) arenaviruses based on their antigenicity, phylogeny, 
and geographical distribution. Many of the NW arenaviruses are highly pathogenic 
viruses that cause systemic human infections characterized by hemorrhagic fever 
and/or neurological manifestations, constituting public health problems in their 
endemic regions. NW arenavirus infection induces a variety of host innate immune 
responses, which could contribute to the viral pathogenesis and/or influence the 
final outcome of virus infection in vitro and in vivo. On the other hand, NW 
arenaviruses have also developed several strategies to counteract the host 
innate immune response. We will review current knowledge regarding the interplay 
between the host innate immune response and NW arenavirus infection in vitro and 
in vivo, with emphasis on viral-encoded proteins and their effect on the type I 
interferon response.
