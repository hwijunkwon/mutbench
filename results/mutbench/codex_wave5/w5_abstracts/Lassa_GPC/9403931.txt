1. Res Virol. 1997 Sep-Oct;148(5):323-31. doi: 10.1016/s0923-2516(97)89128-5.

Glycoprotein-mediated biological properties of a host range mutant of Junin 
virus.

Scolaro LA(1), Damonte EB.

Author information:
(1)Departamento de Química Biológica, Facultad de Ciencias Exactas y Naturales, 
Universidad de Buenos Aires, Ciudad Universitaria.

The biological properties mediated by the main envelope glycoprotein (GP1) of a 
mouse-attenuated mutant of Junin virus, named Cl67, were investigated in 
comparison with its parental strain XJCl3. In contrast to the parental strain, 
this mutant was unable to multiply in primary cultures of mouse embryo 
fibroblasts (MEFs). Impairment of Cl67 multiplication was associated with a lack 
of virus binding to MEF, probably due to an altered interaction between GP1 and 
cellular receptors. Antigenic and immunogenic characterization of GP1, performed 
by neutralization assays, demonstrated that, under certain conditions, 
polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies exhibited differential affinity and 
specificity for each virus. Cl67-infected Vero cells showed a marked 
pH-dependent fusion capability, suggesting more efficient low pH triggering of 
fusion by mutant virus GP1 in comparison with the parental strain.
