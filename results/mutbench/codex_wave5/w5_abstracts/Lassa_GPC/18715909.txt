Identification of a broad-spectrum arenavirus entry inhibitor.

Larson RA(1), Dai D, Hosack VT, Tan Y, Bolken TC, Hruby DE, Amberg SM.

Author information:
(1)SIGA Technologies, Inc., Corvallis, Oregon 97333, USA.

Several arenaviruses, including Lassa virus (LASV), are causative agents of 
hemorrhagic fever, for which effective therapeutic options are lacking. The LASV 
envelope glycoprotein (GP) gene was used to generate lentiviral pseudotypes to 
identify small-molecule inhibitors of viral entry. A benzimidazole derivative 
with potent antiviral activity was identified from a high-throughput screen 
utilizing this strategy. Subsequent lead optimization for antiviral activity 
identified a modified structure, ST-193, with a 50% inhibitory concentration 
(IC(50)) of 1.6 nM against LASV pseudotypes. ST-193 inhibited pseudotypes 
generated with other arenavirus envelopes as well, including the remaining four 
commonly associated with hemorrhagic fever (IC(50)s for Junín, Machupo, 
Guanarito, and Sabiá were in the 0.2 to 12 nM range) but exhibited no antiviral 
activity against pseudotypes incorporating either the GP from the LASV-related 
arenavirus lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) or the unrelated G protein 
from vesicular stomatitis virus, at concentrations of up to 10 microM. 
Determinants of ST-193 sensitivity were mapped through a combination of 
LASV-LCMV domain-swapping experiments, genetic selection of viral variants, and 
site-directed mutagenesis. Taken together, these studies demonstrate that 
sensitivity to ST-193 is dictated by a segment of about 30 amino acids within 
the GP2 subunit. This region includes the carboxy-terminal region of the 
ectodomain and the predicted transmembrane domain of the envelope protein, 
revealing a novel antiviral target within the arenavirus envelope GP.
