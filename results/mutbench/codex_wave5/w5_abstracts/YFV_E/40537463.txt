antibody titers.

Vaccine responses vary across populations and are influenced by numerous 
intrinsic and extrinsic factors, including the gut microbiota. However, studies 
linking microbiota composition to vaccine immunogenicity in low- and 
middle-income countries are sparse. In this study, we examined the gut 
microbiota of 143 healthy rural and urban living Tanzanians who participated in 
a yellow fever vaccine (YF-17D) trial. We found significant differences in gut 
microbiota profiles between rural and urban participants. Rural-associated 
microbiota showed higher diversity and enrichment of taxa like Prevotella and 
Succinivibrio, which were linked to dietary intake patterns. Yellow fever 
neutralizing antibody titers were higher in rural compared to urban 
participants. Interestingly, a subset of urban individuals with a rural-like 
microbiota had higher antibody titers and faster antibody waning than those with 
a more industrialized microbiota. These findings suggest that gut microbiota 
composition might be linked to vaccine immunogenicity, potentially outweighing 
the influence of living location.
