1. J Gen Virol. 2016 May;97(5):1087-1093. doi: 10.1099/jgv.0.000430.

A newly discovered flavivirus in the yellow fever virus group displays 
restricted replication in vertebrates.

Colmant AMG(1), Bielefeldt-Ohmann H(1), Hobson-Peters J(1), Suen WW(1), O'Brien 
CA(1), van den Hurk AF(2), Hall RA(1).

Author information:
(1)Australian Infectious Diseases Research Centre, School of Chemistry and 
Molecular Biosciences, The University of Queensland, St Lucia, Queensland 4072,

(2)Public Health Virology, Forensic and Scientific Services, Department of

A novel flavivirus, provisionally named Bamaga virus (BgV), was isolated from 
Culex annulirostris mosquitoes collected from northern Australia. Phylogenetic 
analysis of the complete nucleotide sequence of the BgV genome revealed it 
clustered with the yellow fever virus (YFV) group, and was most closely related 
to Edge Hill virus (EHV), another Australian flavivirus, with 61.9 % nucleotide 
and 63.7 % amino acid sequence identity. Antigenic analysis of the envelope and 
pre-membrane proteins of BgV further revealed epitopes common to EHV, dengue and 
other mosquito-borne flaviviruses. However, in contrast to these viruses, BgV 
displayed restricted growth in a range of vertebrate cell lines with no or 
relatively slow replication in inoculated cultures. There was also restricted 
BgV replication in virus-challenged mice. Our results indicate that BgV is an 
evolutionary divergent member of the YFV group of flaviviruses, and represents a 
novel system to study mechanisms of virus host-restriction and transmission.
