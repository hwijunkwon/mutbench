1. Virology. 2005 Jul 5;337(2):262-72. doi: 10.1016/j.virol.2005.04.031.

Antibody responses against wild-type yellow fever virus and the 17D vaccine 
strain: characterization with human monoclonal antibody fragments and 
neutralization escape variants.

Daffis S(1), Kontermann RE, Korimbocus J, Zeller H, Klenk HD, Ter Meulen J.

Author information:
(1)Institut für Virologie, Philipps-Universität, Robert-Koch-Strasse 17, 35037 
Marburg, Germany. daffis@mailer.uni-marburg.de

Human monoclonal antibody fragments neutralizing wild-type and vaccine strains 
of yellow fever (YF) virus (genotypes West Africa I + II, East/Central Africa, 
17D-204-WHO) were generated by repertoire cloning from YF patients. Analysis of 
virus escape variants identified amino acid (aa) 71 in domain II of the envelope 
glycoprotein (E) as the most critical residue for neutralization, with aa 
153-155 in domain I contributing to the epitope. These data confirm the previous 
mapping of YFV neutralizing epitopes using mouse monoclonal antibodies but 
suggest that a conformational epitope could be formed by amino acids from 
domains I and II opposing each other in the dimeric form of the E protein. While 
the sera of the YF patients showed up to 10-fold reduced neutralizing activity 
against the 17D escape variants, sera from 17D vaccinees retained their 
neutralizing titers. Mutations in this major neutralizing epitope of YFV thus do 
not seem to carry the risk of immune escape in persons immunized with the 
YFV-17D vaccine.
