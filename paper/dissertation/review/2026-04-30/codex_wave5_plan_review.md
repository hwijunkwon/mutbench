# Codex Wave 5 Plan Review

## Verdict

**PROCEED WITH MODIFICATIONS.** Option A is the right execution boundary for this wave: automated discovery and worksheet generation, hard manual curation, then automated integration and validation. The plan is close, but it needs targeted edits before Task 1 to broaden fixed search coverage, separate DMS-only labels from literature-primary Layer A, make the human checkpoint machine-enforceable, and report validation as `12->15 labeled within 28 unique targets` rather than a new 30-target panel.

## Q1. Search keyword scope

The keyword sets are predeclared and directionally defensible, but they are too narrow for a literature-curation task where missing a known antibody, receptor, or domain term would bias the evidence inventory. The fix is not to let Codex add concepts after seeing results; it is to add a small locked expansion before execution. Add explicit terms for escape mutant/resistant mutant, antibody mapping, glycan shielding, domain nomenclature, and lineage/strain aliases. For YFV/WNV, include flavivirus E domain terms (`EDI`, `EDII`, `EDIII`, fusion loop) and full species aliases. For Lassa, include LASV, arenavirus, SSP, GPC-C, GP-C, GP1, GP2, glycan, neutralizing antibody, and entry/fusion terms. Also require recording zero-hit queries, because zero-hit locked terms are useful evidence against cherry-picking.

## Q2. DMS evidence weighting

DMS escape data should be treated as a sensitivity label set by default, not as primary Layer A evidence, unless a human curator explicitly upgrades a DMS position because it is anchored by literature or a study-recommended high-confidence escape threshold with clear assay relevance. Chapter 3 separates Layer A literature-curated adaptive/diversifying positions from Layer C DMS fitness evidence; merging DMS-only escape into primary Layer A risks changing the target definition mid-dissertation. The plan can still collect and score DMS aggressively, but Task 4 should export `layer_a_primary` and `layer_a_dms_sensitivity` or at least an `evidence_basis` column so the validation can report literature-primary and DMS-inclusive results separately.

## Q3. Manual checkpoint hardness

Task 3 is conceptually strict enough, but it needs a machine-readable lock to prevent accidental auto-resume. Right now the plan says Codex must wait for completed worksheets, but Task 4 validation only checks that `final_label`, `curator`, and `curation_date` are populated; a script could accidentally accept values copied from `proposed_label`. Add an explicit `human_review_status=approved`, `curator_attestation`, or separate approval manifest that Codex cannot generate during Tasks 1-2. Also require Task 2 to leave all human-owned columns blank and Task 4 to fail if `final_label` exactly equals `proposed_label` for all non-empty evidence rows without an explicit curator attestation, because that pattern is a strong accidental auto-fill signature.

## Q4. Coordinate mapping safeguards

The coordinate rules are good but not yet strong enough for polyprotein and precursor numbering. YFV/WNV E evidence can be reported as mature E, polyprotein, prM/E precursor, structural domain residue, or PDB numbering; Lassa GPC can be reported as pre-GPC, signal-peptide-inclusive GPC, GP1, GP2, SSP, or mature subunit numbering. Add explicit reference accession/protein lengths, inferred offsets, cleavage-site assumptions, and a source-to-target coordinate equation to the mapping audit. For each mapped evidence row, require `source_position`, `source_residue`, `target_position`, `target_residue`, `mapping_basis`, `offset`, `aa_mismatch`, and `mapping_confidence`; unresolved or low-confidence mappings must remain out of both primary and sensitivity labels until human approval.

## Q5. Confidence rubric

The confidence rubric needs to distinguish confidence in the biology from confidence in the coordinate mapping and from the evidence basis. A single `confidence` value cannot cleanly encode DMS-only high assay confidence but low Layer-A-definition confidence, or literature-only region evidence with good biological relevance but weak residue precision. Add `evidence_basis` with values such as `literature_primary`, `literature_plus_dms`, `dms_only`, `region_only`, and `coordinate_uncertain`, plus `mapping_confidence` separate from `label_confidence`. Primary validation should use high/medium `label_confidence` literature-primary or literature-plus-DMS positions; DMS-only and broad-region-only positions should be reported as sensitivity unless explicitly upgraded by the curator.

## Q6. Validation framing

Use `12->15 labeled within 28 unique targets` as the formal framing. "30-target stability rerun" is acceptable only as historical shorthand in prose if immediately followed by the exact denominators, but the file names, task title, and report language should avoid implying that Wave 5 adds three new unique targets. The three curated targets already exist inside the Wave 4 28-target universe; their status changes from unlabeled to labeled. The validation question is therefore whether adding up to three curated labeled folds changes labeled LOPO and quorum diagnostics, not whether a 30-unique-target panel has been validated.

## Q7. Failure-mode branches

Branches A/B/C are useful, but Branch A should require literature-primary positives rather than high-confidence positives of any basis, and Branch B should not allow lower-confidence targets to become part of the headline labeled LOPO without clear labeling as sensitivity. Branch C is appropriately conservative. Add a DMS-dominance branch that forces a literature-primary headline plus DMS-inclusive sensitivity, and add a sparse-target rule that prevents "1 successful target plus two weak targets" from being described as a three-target pilot success. Branch G is good and should remain because labels may not fix the Wave 4 stability bottleneck.

## Q8. Codex Q7 ordering

Wave 5 is the right next step after Wave 4, but it does not yet answer the narrowed Wave 4 Q5 claim. Wave 4 showed feature compatibility but only 1/28 stability, so Wave 5 should be framed as testing whether manual labels reduce the most obvious bottleneck, not as proving the 12-pathogen failure was a small-panel artifact. Even if all three targets curate cleanly, `n_labeled_eval` rises only from 12 to at most 15, which is still below the target count needed to make a strong quorum generalization. The expectation should be "pilot evidence for whether curation is worth scaling," not "resolution of Q7."

## Wave 6 ordering recommendation

Wave 6 should be conditional on Wave 5's evidence basis, not just its validation numbers. If at least two targets produce >=10 high-confidence literature-primary positives and labeled LOPO/stability improves, scale Layer A curation to the next small batch of feature-compatible targets before adding new model features. If the pilot is DMS-dominated, sparse, or coordinate-fragile, Wave 6 should be a curation-method repair wave: stronger coordinate references, target-specific curator protocols, and possibly choosing easier antibody-escape targets. EVE/PLM site-effect generation remains valuable but should not be used to compensate for unresolved Layer A definitions.

## Defense-Readiness Risk Re-Scoring

**Medium.** The plan has the right scientific boundary and is much safer than an automated-labeling wave, but defense risk remains medium until DMS-only evidence is split from primary Layer A, the human checkpoint is made machine-enforceable, and the validation denominator is corrected to `12->15 labeled within 28 unique targets`. With those edits, the plan becomes a bounded, defensible pilot rather than a premature expanded-panel validation claim.
