### Area 2 -- cluster bootstrap with G=11
The small-cluster limitation is adequately disclosed: the text states that 1,000 pathogen-level cluster-bootstrap resamples over 11 pathogens yield [0.195, 0.333] (chapters_en/ch4_results.tex:523), contrasts this with a narrower naive bootstrap [0.260, 0.321] (chapters_en/ch4_results.tex:524), and explicitly says wild-cluster bootstrap is recommended future work for G <= 30 (chapters_en/ch4_results.tex:524). I found no paper giving a reliable generic CI-width inflation factor for percentile vs wild-cluster at G=11; Cameron/Gelbach/Miller report few-cluster over-rejection and that bootstrap-t can reduce 10% rejection to nominal 5%, and Roodman/MacKinnon/Nielsen/Webb describe wild cluster bootstrap as preferable when large-sample assumptions fail with few clusters. The dissertation's caveat is therefore adequate. Leave-one-pathogen point estimates I computed with the archived manual omega path stayed above 0.25; the body already discloses the harsher same-evidence-type 4-pathogen restriction at omega^2 = 0.137, just below Cohen's large threshold (chapters_en/ch5_discussion.tex:292).

External sources consulted: Cameron et al. 2008, https://www.nber.org/papers/t0344; Roodman et al. 2019, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1536867X19830877.
