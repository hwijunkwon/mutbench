## 2. Strongest expected defense counter-attack

The committee counter-attack should be: "You are confusing existence of scattered escape literature with a comparable, curated, residue-level, non-circular antibody-escape validation panel. The manuscript predeclares why the other eight were excluded and does not claim absence of all annotations." The manuscript's current best response is Chapter 4's availability paragraph, which classifies the eight exclusions by granularity, sample size, Layer-A confounding, and mechanism mismatch, then explicitly says the validated pathogens are a convenience sample rather than a balanced subset (paper/dissertation/chapters_en/ch4_results.tex:949). The second strong response is the three-tier evidence rule: HIV-1 alone passes both Bonferroni and Layer-A independence; H3N2 and SARS-CoV-2 are demoted (paper/dissertation/chapters_en/ch5_discussion.tex:114-119).

## 3. Recommended manuscript additions

1. Replace "annotation availability" in the abstract with "availability of comparable curated residue-level antibody-escape panels meeting the predeclared non-circularity and sample-size criteria." This closes the easy objection that external annotations exist for the other eight pathogens.

2. Add a compact eight-row supplement table listing excluded pathogens, broad external annotation existence, exclusion reason, and whether a future curation pass could make them eligible.

3. In the search-space-reduction paragraph, qualify "7--9x enrichment" as "observed on the three available escape panels, with independent external support primarily from HIV-1." This prevents the typical-protein workflow from sounding all-panel validated.

## 4. Verdict

Contribution 3 has medium vulnerability to a cherry-pick attack. The manuscript has already neutralised the most damaging version by identifying HIV-1 as the only clean external anchor, demoting H3N2/SARS-CoV-2, and admitting convenience-sample limits. The remaining vulnerability is terminological and presentational: "availability" is too broad, because external annotations exist for the excluded pathogens, even if most are not comparable enough for the current enrichment protocol.
