## 2. Identified fragile assumptions in current statistical claims

1. "The large sample size (N = 8,580) ensures ANOVA robustness" is too strong. The manuscript immediately acknowledges the effective independent unit is closer to 3,080 cells or 11 pathogen clusters. Robustness should be attributed to the cluster/wild/phylogenetic/mixed/Bayesian checks, not to nominal N alone.

2. "Largest source" is fragile unless always qualified as "largest modeled component." The residual component is about 0.39 and exceeds the scoring x pathogen omega = 0.296. The current text mostly handles this, but any abstract/summary wording should preserve the qualifier.

3. The fixed-effect omega is sensitive to the evaluation-level vs cell-level unit choice. The manuscript does disclose that 0.296 falls to 0.234 at cell level. Claims should avoid implying 0.296 is the conservative cross-pathogen estimand.

4. Normality and homoscedasticity are formally rejected. The conclusion is not highly fragile because ART and bootstrap checks exist, but claims about exact p-values or precise variance shares are assumption-sensitive.

5. IID within pathogen is not defensible for detector variants sharing the same MSA and ground truth. The cluster bootstrap helps, but the point estimate remains an evaluation-grid decomposition, not a count of independent biological trials.

6. Pathogen exchangeability is limited. The 11 pathogens differ in alignment length, MSA depth, positive rate, viral family, and Layer-A evidence type. HCV removal and covariate adjustment help, but the interaction still partially confounds pathogen biology with curation-protocol heterogeneity.

7. Cohen's omega thresholds are borrowed from behavioral science. The manuscript notes this; any defense claim should say "large by Cohen's conventional threshold" rather than "large in an externally calibrated computational-benchmark sense."

8. LOPO 0/11 and the 0.265 gap are weak if read alone. The text now correctly says the match rate is null-consistent and the gap is not statistically distinguishable from a random-selection null. This must remain framed as corroborative, not independent proof.

9. Bootstrap CIs with 11 pathogen clusters and 8 ICTV-family blocks have limited calibration. Wild-cluster and Bayesian checks reduce concern, but interval endpoints should not be overinterpreted to three decimals.

10. The ART check supports the interaction but has two reported estimates (0.277 and 0.343). That discrepancy is not conclusion-threatening, but it is fragile as a reproducibility claim.

## 3. Recommended addition

Add C. pathogen-blocked permutation ANOVA, and add nothing else before defense.

Rationale: it is cheap, directly addresses the examiner's likely "what is the null randomization?" objection, and does not introduce a new outcome definition. The blocked permutation should preserve pathogen block structure and recompute the same Type-II-like omega statistic under a clearly stated label-shuffling scheme. This gives the largest robustness gain per hour. Logistic/binomial and Bayesian distributional averaging are potentially interesting but would open definitional disputes over the endpoint. Random-slope GLMM and robust regression are unlikely to change the conclusion given the existing mixed, Bayesian, LOO, HCV-exclusion, and ART results.
