\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This chapter summarizes the research contributions of MutBench, analyzes its limitations, and presents future research directions.

\section{Summary of Research Contributions}
\label{sec:contribution_summary}

This dissertation contributes three results.
\textbf{First}, MutBench provides a benchmark for viral mutation hotspot detection across 11 RNA viruses, combining 20 scoring formulas, 39 detector variants, and a three-layer ground truth (literature-curated adaptive positives, constrained negatives, and DMS fitness where available).
\textbf{Second}, the benchmark shows that the optimal biological information source is pathogen-dependent: the scoring~$\times$~pathogen interaction is the largest modeled effect ($\omega^2=0.296$ at 20-type granularity; 0.103 at 6-category granularity), the 11 pathogens have 9 distinct optimal information types, and the oracle-vs-generalized MCC gap is 0.265.
Friedman $\chi^2=7.69$, $p=0.990$ shows no universally best combination across pathogens; LOPO 0/11 is null-consistent under permutation; both serve as corroborative consistency checks rather than independent evidence.
\textbf{Third}, multi-source integration supplies a practical cold-start floor: the 4-feature core (homoplasy, pLDDT, entropy, frequency) retains 97.6\% of the 10-feature EqualWeight ensemble mean MCC (numerical equivalence under uniform-weight LOPO top-10\%; no paired-test significance), and the H3N2 integration case yields 4.012-fold escape enrichment.
External validation is anchored by HIV-1, where the escape set is largely Layer~A-disjoint (82\%) and yields full-set 7.19-fold enrichment ($p_{\text{adj}} = 2.5 \times 10^{-16}$) and a Layer~A-disjoint novel-only enrichment of 7.16--8.24$\times$ (worst-case Fisher $p = 5.0 \times 10^{-12}$); H3N2 is a self-consistency check (69\% Layer~A overlap) and SARS-CoV-2 is exploratory after multiple-comparison correction.
The PAHD-R redesign translates these findings into a conservative evidence-fusion workflow, confirming the feasibility and audit boundaries of adaptive extension without claiming a completed universal adaptive predictor.

\textbf{Pathogen-Aware Adaptive Method Selection.}

The oracle ceiling (MCC 0.160 vs.\ EqualWeight 0.083) shows that adaptive gains are possible, but 11 reference pathogens are insufficient for reliable selection in a 13-dimensional profile space.
PAHD-R is therefore positioned as an auditable intermediate synthesis: Core, Augmented, and Review are adopted global modes; HCV E2 is the only adopted optional repair; SARS-CoV-2, MERS, calibrated, shrinkage, virus-aware, and selective variants remain future-validation candidates.
The next step is not further tuning on the same 9-pathogen PAHD-R panel, but expansion to larger and time-forward validation panels followed by explicit meta-learning or algorithm-selection models~\cite{rice1976algorithm,kerschke2019algorithm}.

\textbf{Integration of Phylogenetic Correction.}

Homoplasy-based scoring captures signals distinct from frequency (simulation $\rho=-0.876$; real GISAID $\rho=-0.107$) and should be integrated through TreeTime~\cite{sagulenko2018treetime} or scalable alternatives such as UShER~\cite{turakhia2020ultrafast}.
This would mainly affect founder-effect-prone pathogens such as SARS-CoV-2, and should be paired with temporal/geographic stratification.

\textbf{Additional Research Directions.}

Additional directions are DNA-virus extension, multi-protein evaluation, indel-aware scoring, virus-tuned PLM features, and real-time surveillance integration.

\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:final_conclusion}

MutBench shows that viral hotspot detection is driven more by the fit between pathogen and biological information source than by detector choice alone.
Within the single-position scoring regime and modeled ANOVA terms, the scoring~$\times$~pathogen interaction ($\omega^2=0.296$) is the largest identified factor, and nine distinct optimal information types appear across 11 pathogens.
The practical validation is strongest for HIV-1 escape enrichment (7.19$\times$, 82\% Layer~A-disjoint), while H3N2 is mainly self-consistency evidence and SARS-CoV-2 remains exploratory.
The 4-feature core and PAHD-R workflow convert these findings into usable prioritization tools, but the adaptive claim remains bounded: the dissertation confirms a feasible direction for adaptive evidence fusion, not a universal adaptive predictor.
\textbf{Take-home message}: choosing the right biological information source for the pathogen explains more performance variation than choosing among detection algorithms, and MutBench quantifies that asymmetry.

\paragraph{Dual-use considerations.} MutBench predicts positions that are likely to mutate under selection, including immune-escape-relevant sites on viral surface glycoproteins. The same outputs that inform vaccine-strain selection, surveillance triage, and antibody design can in principle inform engineering of escape variants, so the benchmark outputs are inherently dual-use. Three mitigations were therefore built into the release plan rather than being deferred to downstream users: (i)~raw GISAID sequences are never redistributed---only computed H-scores, detection results, and summary statistics are shared, preserving the provider-side access controls of the underlying data agreement; (ii)~the benchmark publishes \emph{region-level} hotspot rankings, not variant-level engineering recipes, so it raises the signal-to-noise for surveillance without providing a direct uplift to escape-variant design; and (iii)~the public repository release (30~days post-defence) coincides with disclosure to the relevant biosafety review body, and any extension of the benchmark to pathogens on the U.S.\ HHS P3CO/Select Agent lists will go through a dedicated institutional biosafety review prior to publication. These steps do not eliminate dual-use risk, but they keep the benchmark aligned with the \emph{responsible stewardship} expectations now standard for computational pathogen-evolution work.

\paragraph{Code and data availability.}\label{sec:code_availability}
The MutBench source code, scripts, ground truth definitions, and configuration files are archived at \url{https://github.com/hwijunkwon/mutbench} under tag \texttt{dissertation-v1} (SHA and Zenodo DOI to be inserted at deposit).%
\footnote{During review, the tagged snapshot is available in the supplementary bundle; public Zenodo mirroring is scheduled within 30~days of defense.}
The benchmark can be reproduced from three archived components:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item feature-computation scripts listed in the bundled provenance manifest;
\item Stage~3 detector grid: \path{python run_stage3_full_detectors.py};
\item statistical analysis: \path{python analyze_stage3_stats.py}.
\end{itemize}
With precomputed features, the full 8{,}580-evaluation detector grid runs in approximately 25 seconds on a single workstation.
The environment used Python~3.10, NumPy/SciPy/Pandas, MAFFT, IQ-TREE, HyPhy, and ESM-2; stochastic analyses used seed 42.
Raw GISAID sequences are not redistributed; only computed scores, detections, and summaries are shared.
